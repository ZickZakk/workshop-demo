# Chapter 1: The Basics of Soccer

Soccer, called football in most of the world, is the most popular sport on the planet. More than 200 countries play it, and a World Cup final draws over a billion viewers.

## The objective

Two teams try to get the ball into the opposing goal. Players may use any part of their body except their hands and arms. The team with more goals at the end of the match wins. If the score is level, the match is a draw, unless a winner is required. Then extra time and a penalty shoot-out follow.

## Teams and positions

Each team has eleven players on the pitch:

- **Goalkeeper:** the only player allowed to handle the ball, and only inside their own penalty area
- **Defenders:** protect their own goal and win the ball back
- **Midfielders:** connect defence and attack and control the tempo
- **Forwards:** create chances and score goals

## Match duration

A match lasts 90 minutes, split into two halves of 45 minutes with a 15-minute break. The referee adds stoppage time at the end of each half for time lost to injuries, substitutions and other delays.

## Substitutions

A **substitution** is when a team replaces a player on the pitch with a **substitute**, a team member waiting off the pitch. Teams make substitutions to replace tired or injured players, or to change how they play. A substitute may enter only when play has stopped, the referee has given permission and the player being replaced has left the pitch. Most senior competitions allow each team to use up to five substitutes, but the limit depends on the competition. A player who has been replaced normally cannot return to the same match, although some youth and amateur competitions allow this.

## The pitch and the goal

The pitch is a rectangle of grass or artificial turf, 100 to 110 metres long and 64 to 75 metres wide for international matches. Each goal is 7.32 metres wide and 2.44 metres high.

## Fouls and cards

Kicking, tripping, pushing or holding an opponent is a foul. The other team gets a free kick, or a penalty kick if the foul happens inside the penalty area. For serious or repeated fouls, the referee shows a **yellow card** as a warning. A second yellow card, or a **red card** for a very serious foul, sends the player off for the rest of the match.
